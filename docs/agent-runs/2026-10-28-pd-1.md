## Rulings

- Ruling: built by hand, test-first, with no `/build`, no planner second opinion and no subagents. I applied the edge-case-hunter, typescript-reviewer and silent-failure-hunter checklists myself. - why: coordinator override. - cost if wrong: the coordinator's review finds what a dispatched reviewer would have found.
- Ruling: `PlanItem.step` is the 1-based position in the plan, and `kind` names the rule. - why: matches the "01".."05" column in design.md §8. - cost if wrong: the screen derives the number itself.
- Ruling: the overdue step always runs first, whatever `plannerStepOrder` says. The other three steps follow the configured order, which must be a permutation of all four. - why: PRD §5.3 acceptance says "an overdue card always appears first", and the order is configurable. - cost if wrong: an admin can't move the overdue step.
- Ruling: when cash runs short on minimums, the card where the cash runs out gets part of its minimum. The shortfall records `required`, `funded` and `shortfall`; later cards show `funded: 0`. - why: the Rohan example in the IA file ("ICICI ₹1,550 of ₹2,880, will miss by ₹1,330"). - cost if wrong: one branch to change.
- Ruling: the cycle's due date is `null` when the remaining statement is 0. This covers both encodings of "paid in full": a statement balance of 0, or `paidSinceStatement` ≥ the statement balance. - why: SBI "no due date this cycle", and PD-4 may encode it either way. - cost if wrong: Home shows "no due" for a fully paid card.
- Ruling: date rules.
  - The last statement is the most recent `statementDay` on or before today, clamped to the month end. A statement generated today counts.
  - The next statement is strictly after today.
  - The due date is the first `dueDay` (clamped) strictly after the last statement.
  - Overdue means `today > dueDate` and `paidSinceStatement < minimumDue`.
  - why: PRD §5.3 and the Aarav dates (HDFC due 4 Nov, Axis due 22 Oct). - cost if wrong: an off-by-one-cycle on the statement day itself.
- Ruling: the weekly snapshots are the 7th, 14th, 21st, 28th and month-end, strictly after today. Non-leap 28 Feb counts once, and the 3rd ("full file", F2) is not a reporting point. - why: PRD §5.4 lists exactly those days. - cost if wrong: one constant.
- Ruling: pay-by = reporting point − `payByBufferDays` (default 1), never before today. A negative or fractional buffer throws. - why: PRD §5.4 "one day before", buffer configurable; Aarav SBI 25 Oct gives 24 Oct. - cost if wrong: none for the demo.
- Ruling: under `weekly_current`, step-3 and step-4 pay-by = the earlier of (due date, snapshot − buffer), clamped to today. Minimum pay-by stays the due date. - why: PRD §5.4 moves only steps 3 and 4. - cost if wrong: with this rule a statement can be due to be paid before its own minimum (HDFC, 27 Oct vs 4 Nov). That follows the PRD literally but may read oddly on screen.
- Ruling: step 4 is sequential greedy, the literal PRD text. The highest-utilization card is paid down to the target, then the next. It is not water-filling. - why: PRD §5.3 4(a) wording; it gives the same result whenever the cash suffices. - cost if wrong: when cash is short, the highest card can end above what levelling would give.
- Ruling: there is one `prepay` item per card across phases (a) and (b). - why: design.md §8 shows SBI as a single ₹26,400 step. - cost if wrong: none.
- Ruling: the threshold cap is `floor(limit × pct / 100)` whole rupees, and "above the threshold" compares exact rupees, never the rounded display value. - why: 30.04% shows as 30.0% but is still above (tested). - cost if wrong: none.
- Ruling: step 3 orders cards by known interest rate (highest first), then cards with no known rate, then by due date, then by id. - why: PRD §5.3 step 3. - cost if wrong: an unknown-rate card might deserve priority.
- Ruling: a statement still unpaid after its due date is planned for today, with reason `statement_after_due`. - why: interest is already accruing, so paying it is still the right step-3 action. - cost if wrong: copy only.
- Ruling: a negative outstanding (a credit balance) is treated as 0, and a paid-since figure larger than the statement is floored. Malformed input throws `RangeError`. Malformed means: fractional, negative or NaN rupees; impossible dates; days outside 1-31; duplicate ids; a bad step order; percentages outside 0-100. - why: Mark as paid can produce the first two; the rest are caller bugs that Zod boundaries catch (docs/standards.md "Errors"; the `format.ts` precedent). - cost if wrong: a screen must validate before calling.
- Ruling: SBI's fixture `dueDay` is 14. - why: the IA gives none; the mockup's label says 14 Nov. It never shows, because nothing is owed. - cost if wrong: none.
- Ruling: a percent inside a reason drops a trailing `.0` ("30%", "18.7%"). - why: matches the IA copy "₹36,000 (30%)". - cost if wrong: copy only.
- Ruling: the only shortfalls are unfunded minimums. A part-paid statement is an item with `statement_partial`, not a shortfall. - why: PRD §5.3 defines a shortfall against minimums. - cost if wrong: the screen can't list interest-bearing remainders separately.
